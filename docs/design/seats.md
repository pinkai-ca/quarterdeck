# Quarterdeck design: Seats

A page of [the design](../DESIGN.md).

## 6. Seats

A seat is one running program in one pane, under one role. `qd up <seat>` launches it with what its adapter builds: the pool's credential, the model and effort, the brief and the seat's settings, all as flags and overrides on the program's own command line, with the seat's open messages as the first message where the program takes one there. `qd up` with no seat brings up the ship and normally resumes each seat that has a record. `qd down <seat>` ends one. Restart and fresh are one relaunch path with one difference: `qd restart <seat>` relaunches with resume, keeping the conversation, for a control that has only a launch form or a program that has stopped answering; `qd fresh <seat>` relaunches without resume, a new conversation that begins with the brief and the open messages. Nothing is typed to clear a seat, because a typed clear cannot change a seat's pool or program, leaves a second record in its home, and cannot refresh a brief that was passed at launch. The seat's current record is the first record written after its launch, which is exact because one process writes a home; older records stay for the Ledger.

`Seat.lookup_record()` validates only an existing record pin and its reader, without discovering a candidate or writing ownership, launch history, folders or locks. Ordinary callers keep using `record()`: it takes that read-only path only when the reader binding, owned-record entry and exact current launch boundary are already retained. Otherwise `join()` still pins the first record after launch and journals its ownership; ambiguous candidates remain reported, never chosen. Callers needing `Join.reason` use the same retention check through `join()`. Explicit lifecycle joins still retain missing evidence, including a resumed record's new pool boundary.

For a down seat declared `fresh = "after-order"`, `qd up` instead starts fresh when its last delivered order has its addressee's closing report and no work remains open. An acknowledgment or another seat's report does not close an order. The decision uses `Store.open_for()` in the same read-only post view as the service's `fresh_after_order` decision; an open question also retains the conversation. Queued or unconfirmed attempts alone grant no delivery proof. The decision view ends before the normal fresh launch builds its brief and carriage, so newly arrived messages are still carried. The proof adds `fresh: its last order is reported`. A seat with open work, no delivered order, or no after-order policy keeps normal resume behavior; explicit restart/fresh behavior is unchanged.

**A thread index after restart.** `[ship] carry_bytes` is a positive integer, default 100,000 actual UTF-8 bytes. It bounds the first message and each generated index page, as ship policy rather than measured program capacity. Doctor shows its value in force. There is no environment override, token ratio, automatic halving or adapter measurement claim. The first message is also kept inside the existing kernel-oriented inline budget; final argv/environment checks still run after adapter and jail encoding.

Every actual fresh or resumed conversation receives its standing brief and an index by connected post thread. An empty index has zero lines and adds no model turn; the native brief still loads. A thread is present when it contains an open incoming order/question, an undelivered incoming report/answer/queued message, or an open order/question sent by the seat. The latter is marked `sent, awaiting ADDRESSEE` and creates no duty or delivery attempt. Incoming unread replies remain under their ancestry's one thread line. Each line names the thread, sender, subject, due time, relevant active ids, the messages amending it and the `qd thread ID` command. Subjects beyond the existing 200-byte display budget are visibly summarized; their full immutable text remains in the thread. Large indexes have bounded range-routing pages; large amendment/id lists have bounded metadata pages. Nothing but metadata is copied for ordinary messages. Existing launch attempts retain result `launch` with an explicit indexed/not-confirmed-read detail; indexing grants no native-input consumption receipt and changes no closure rule. Index-only and held/unconfirmed posts remain eligible until existing positive delivery/watch or queued-consumption evidence; an earlier valid receipt is not hidden by a later index reference. Metadata reads never settle their attempts.

Only an actual fresh conversation also receives its admitted, non-context-only self-addressed orders in full, before the index. A requested resume that resolves fresh gets the handoff; a real resume does not retype it. A handoff too large for the first message goes whole into one private immutable `handoff.txt`, named first. It may exceed carry_bytes: the first message then says `your handoff is N bytes, over carry_bytes; write a shorter one next time`. Every other generated file still fits the policy. No general whole-carriage body dump or ordinary-message read-view copies are produced.

The index tells the seat to read a thread whole before acting on any message in it. `qd thread ID` follows ancestry to the root and prints every reply once, parents before children and otherwise chronological, with an end marker, as far as the reader sent or received it: a message keeps its body only where the reader is its sender or its addressee. Every other message of the thread is one line naming its kind and id, with no body, no sender's words and no subject, so a forum reviewer never reads a peer's findings and a refused text never reaches another seat of its thread. The index's amendments, waiting and withheld lists likewise hold only the seat's own messages, and a root it neither sent nor received gives no subject. A recovery continuation already delivered to the reader is printed with one fixed line above it, `a recovery continuation for an earlier conversation of this seat; it is not addressed to this conversation`: labelled, never withheld. It also renders as JSON through the ordinary reading interface. It preserves each stored body, including trailing whitespace, is read-only and grants no receipt. Ordinary transport keeps its historical canonical formatting. Existing per-reader withheld notices apply to the index and thread output, without copying an excluded subject or body. Missing ancestry and cycles are explicit. Original posts and their openness remain unchanged. Complete private generations are atomically staged and atomically renamed under the seat's existing carried folder; earlier generations are never rewritten. An impossible minimum account/index or publication failure starts no window. The law guarantees the instruction and full-thread command, rather than claiming code can observe whether an agent obeyed. Fixture presence never marks an installation proved; that is `qd check`.

**Controls.** `qd set <seat> model=claude-opus-5-5 effort=high` changes a seat. It writes the ship file first, and commits the write when the ship folder is a repository, so the change persists through down, up and restart; then it hands the change to the service, which applies it to the running program by the control's in-session form, a slash command typed through the door, so the change stands in the pane where the human can read it, and confirms it by the program's resulting state. A control with no in-session form is written and reported, not forced: `settings written; qd restart scott applies them`. A change of pool is a restart with resume, because the record lives in the seat's own home and only the credential changes: `pool written; qd restart pike applies it`. A change of harness is a fresh start by nature, because the conversation lives with the program: `harness written; qd fresh pike applies it`. The keys of one `qd set` are written as one edit, validated together and committed once, so a seat declared with one program switches program and pool together, `qd set pike harness=claude pool=claude-primary model=claude-opus-5-5`, which neither key could do alone. What may be typed into a person's own session, and the waking line, is [the door](door.md#the-door). `qd controls <seat>` lists a seat's controls with the form each has. A slash command typed by hand in a pane changes the program and not the ship file: the ship file says what should be, the Watch says what is, and `qd status` marks any difference, whether a change is waiting for a restart or a hand changed something in the pane: `model claude-fable-5-1 (ship file: claude-opus-5-5)`. `qd set <seat> --adopt` writes what the seat is running into the file in one command. Settings the framework does not name pass through: the keys of `[seats.<name>.settings]` are the program's own, given verbatim as launch overrides, so a program's new feature is available the day it ships, with no change to the adapter.

**Fresh starts.** Compaction after compaction leaves a context made of summaries of summaries. The framework's guarantee is narrow and kept: after any restart or fresh start a seat receives its brief and every open message in full, except a message its provider refused, which it receives as a notice without the text while the sender is told. What a seat holds only in its context, it loses on a fresh start, so what must survive goes in a post, and the ship's orders say so. `qd fresh <seat>` is a fresh start on demand, and `fresh = "after-order"` in the ship file makes one each time a seat's last open order is closed and the closing turn has ended and been recorded, so every order begins on a clean context. The first report that actually closes the last order must fall within an explicitly completed turn in the active conversation; a duplicate report, an idle screen, a child session or an inferred accounting boundary is not proof. Missing or unreadable completion evidence holds the fresh start, as does an open turn or queued input. It is for workers, who take one order at a time; command seats are long-lived and keep their context, and what a human types straight into a command seat's pane, outside the post, is theirs to lose. The Drill proves the guarantee by relaunching every seat fresh between rounds.

**Historical full-input compatibility.** The retained `thread_context.select` helper defines the pre-index full-input snapshot for historical native-input matching and current self-handoff admission; it does not describe automatic body replay by the new launch index. It selects each canonical open order or question followed by what amends it, following explicit `re` links in timestamp/id order. In a root's thread, a message addressed to the seat amends it when it is an answer whose nearest message from the seat, below the root, is the seat's own question, whoever sent it; or when the root's own sender sent it, of any kind but an acknowledgment or a question, unless it is a report or answer replying directly to an order the seat gave. A question never amends: one still open and addressed to the seat is a root of its own, and one the seat has answered is settled. Open orders and questions inside a thread are roots of their own, each judged by its own sender, and stay in thread order. The rest of a thread is history: chiefly the reports and answers that closed or answered the seat's own sub-orders and the questions the seat has answered, which stay in the inbox and the store and are not carried. The rule reads the graph and the senders alone, never a body. Unread and queued messages are carried whatever their kind, so nothing a seat has not yet received is left out. Each message appears once across open threads, unread posts and queued work. The seat's own outgoing questions appear only when needed to explain an incoming answer; they are marked context only and receive no delivery attempt, receipt or claim. A report from `ship` addressed to the seat directly on an open root is also an amendment only when its exact published id and envelope metadata match that structure's saved terminal notice: the group's non-null notice in `runtime/post/groups/<root>.json`, addressed to its lead on the saved order, or the cascade's final intent in `runtime/ring/<root>/state.json`, addressed to its orchestrator on the saved question with status `done` or `stopped`. Selection checks journal format, root and role consistency, and the group's copy and failed-member metadata against immutable posts; the saved group notice is the writer's terminal marker, without a second completion rule. It never reads, compares, hashes or interprets a body to decide this exception, and never calls the structures' full body-comparing validators. The checked journal read is capped at 1 MiB before parsing; oversized evidence or JSON nesting beyond the parser limit is unknown. Missing, unreadable, malformed, inconsistent or competing structure evidence grants no exception and creates no repair or derived artifact. Every other direct ship report is history, including recovery continuations on the same structure root and reports on a root whose sender is itself `ship`; nested reports and reports to another seat get no terminal-notice exception. Unread or queued reports still arrive once through ordinary extra carriage. Other participants' bodies are not carried. Excluded bodies remain absent from both incoming and explanatory context; open roots remain open, and the existing bodyless notice names a withheld incoming message. An unreadable exclusion journal holds the launch.

Only the actual resolved native resume id permits pending-input deduplication against the pinned owned record being resumed. A requested resume that resolves fresh cannot use an older conversation's consumption to hide pending work. The current index still references its complete reader-visible thread; no ordinary amendment body is automatically replayed. Deduplication recognizes complete canonical native inputs: full incoming envelopes, exact own-question context, the canonical briefed header and bodyless notices. Unknown or truncated surroundings and ambiguous tokenization invalidate the whole input; envelope-looking text within a body is not separate consumption. Only timestamped accepted or validly consumed input qualifies. Headers, context and notices yield no incoming ids. A launch receipt remains carriage evidence, and is never promoted to record delivery by this selection.

`fresh_after_compactions = 2` is an optional framework setting in `[seats.NAME]`. An absent field or `0` disables it; positive integers enable it, and booleans, negatives, strings and floats are refused. The current owned record supplies the count: Claude Code's root `system/compact_boundary` is a completed transcript boundary; Codex's top-level `compacted` is a persisted history checkpoint. Progress, summaries, sidechains and Codex's `context_compacted` notice do not count again. The adapter must prove the native origin and readable history. The seat retains a byte-prefix proof and the native conversation identity: a same-record resume or service restart retains the count, and only a different owned native origin following a proved fresh launch resets it. Incomplete, rewritten, missing, mixed or unsupported history is shown as unknown; the service does not guess a join from other records. Count, execution proof and activity are separate readings. After a proved fresh launch, detailed readings say `compaction count pending: new conversation not yet joined` until the new record joins, then await its compaction observation; they never present the predecessor’s cached count or manual readiness as current. A normal pending join has no operational alarm; an ambiguous or unreadable join remains unknown, and the existing owned-record observation alone can update the count or arm automation.

At threshold, the service saves a fixed immutable request intent before publication, once per generation. The seat posts an open self-addressed order replying to that request (`qd post order --to SELF --re REQUEST --body-file handoff.txt`), which is reserved before publication and kept out of the ending program's delivery/watch. For automatic fresh, the seat invokes `qd fresh-ready --request REQUEST --handoff HANDOFF --at-break` as its last tool act. This attests no review, signature or landing is half done and promises to finish the turn without starting another task. The command itself persists/publishes the seat's fixed ordinary report, then submits a control naming that exact report and revision. Report prose, publication alone, a timeout or an idle screen cannot arm it. The service does not close either obligation on the seat's behalf. Retry of the same revision reuses the exact immutable bytes; later work requires a new handoff and revision.

The service binds readiness to the live pane/PID, native record/conversation, exact input/tool frontier, configuration, incoming post frontier and open handoff bytes. Only that turn's recorded completion and a proved empty composer, record queue and tool set can claim fresh; unresolved queue identity, recovery, unread or unconfirmed work holds it. Work admitted before the claim invalidates readiness. Admission and claim share a recipient lock; after the claim later arrivals are held for the successor, and the lock is released before retirement/startup. The existing lifecycle carries every open message and unread input, including the handoff, with the brief. A crash leaves a saved claim; a proved new launch/record is adopted, and ambiguity is visible without another automatic attempt.

Admission locks follow the lifecycle claim, then recipient names in sorted order, then attempt publication. A report takes the addressee (its sender) and recipient locks, so addressing a closure elsewhere cannot close a reserved handoff. Explicit pending or claimed lifecycle controls hold automatic fresh; their writers release admission after atomic publication, before waiting for the service reply. The retiring delivery/watch filter is not used for successor carriage: the launch snapshots ordinary unread posts, including non-open answers/reports, as well as open work. Arrivals after that snapshot wait for normal delivery to the successor. Fixed threshold request ids remain in the journal across generations. Reports on those requests or their related self-handoffs close them normally, but cannot trigger `after-order` fresh; genuine orders, including unrelated self-orders, keep their existing eligibility.

When execution is unknown at threshold, status says **ready for manual fresh: execution unknown** even before a readiness attempt. This describes the available procedure, not an idle break or execution clearance. The request tells the seat to leave its open self-handoff. An outside controller can run `qd fresh-ready NAME --cancel` even for a down or spent seat, then choose the actual idle break and use explicit `qd fresh NAME`. That is the current path for opaque Codex records: a returned Code Mode call, later completion or attestation cannot prove all nested work ended. In the isolated Codex 0.166.0 direct profile a native terminal result and a yielded process handle are distinguishable, but full native records still contain unclassified `item_completed` events and read execution unknown. Managed tool profiles and execution guards are unchanged. A recognized complete direct-tool shape is eligible only with the required native proofs. Humans receive the threshold notice through their existing watch and remain manual for both compaction fresh and after-order fresh. Automatic wall routing retains its resume/fallback policy and uses the shared known-idle proof described below.

Ordinary restart, down, stop, fresh and failover keep their existing guards. Before disarming a claimed automatic transition, the lifecycle claim adopts its proved successor from the still-current fresh launch and owned record, so an immediate resume retains the new generation and count. Missing or ambiguous successor proof remains unknown and does not veto explicit recovery. The lifecycle claim then disarms readiness and releases the reservation before ordinary carriage, without deleting or closing posts. A same-conversation resume keeps the request and count; preparing automatic readiness again needs a current handoff/frontier. Cancel or disable also releases reservation. The optional versioned `runtime/seats/NAME/compaction.json` stores prefix digests, identity and fixed pending intents, without changing immutable post, attempt or ledger formats. An unreadable/unknown journal holds the automation; ordinary explicit recovery remains available. Before rollback set the threshold to `0`, cancel any remaining preparation and resolve an outstanding transition, then remove `fresh_after_compactions` from the declaration before using the older framework. Retain the journal/posts as evidence. Older code does not enforce this protocol's admission/reservations and must not be used alongside a claimed transition.

An idle seat with a reading of **ready for manual fresh**, or released readiness at or past its threshold, raises a report from `ship` to every systems seat that is up, excluding itself. Idle for this notification means the current owned record explicitly says its turn is closed, with a known empty, error-free queue and no known pending tools. Unknown tool execution is admissible for this notification alone; an unknown record or turn is held, and there is no screen-idle fallback. The report names the seat, the exact safe compaction reading and any open self-handoffs linked by routing metadata to its compaction requests, or says none is open. It includes no handoff body. The outside controller still chooses the break under the ship's orders: the report supplies no fresh, teardown or execution clearance.

The existing compaction pass reconciles these reports without another loop. The optional `runtime/seats/NAME/manual-fresh-alert.json` retains the standing generation/source/reading, its first observed handoff metadata in a fixed report body, and immutable report intents. The saved names and bytes precede publication; restart recovers the same reports, including publication interrupted by a compaction generation change. Once is per recipient and standing safe reading, independent of record growth or handoff inventory changes. A newly up systems recipient gets one report with that first observed text; an already told recipient returning after downtime gets none. A cleared compaction reading or changed generation/source/reading rearms; busy turns, queued input, running tools and unknown evidence hold the standing state, and a clear missed during service downtime is never inferred. Saved unpublished observations are recovered even if the reading has since cleared. This journal changes no post, attempt or ledger format, and changing handoff inventory alone never rearms it. Released readiness includes new incoming work, stale readiness or attestation, explicit cancellation, declaration change and ordinary lifecycle release; a newly posted handoff waiting for initial readiness alone does not qualify.


When the person has stopped, and the order that tells the desk to start fresh, are [the door](door.md#the-door), with the waking line.

**Turns that end in an error.** An adapter reads a turn's recorded error into the turn and the seat's state (law: record-first): its kind, the native code verbatim where the record names one, its first sixteen words with control characters removed, the inputs the turn took, and whether the model produced output, which is true on any model text, reasoning, tool call or positive output count, false only when every row of the turn is known and complete and the record states no final message, and unknown otherwise. A later turn clears it from the state; the turn keeps it, and the Ledger's turn is unchanged. Kinds: a `provider refusal` is only a native form the adapter positively classifies as the provider refusing the conversation; any other recorded error is a `provider error` or a `program error`. A refusal is never an allowance wall. A wall is read from a turn error only in a usage-limit form its adapter names ([section 9](ledger.md#9-ledger), Ledger); every other turn error is no wall.

The door holds while the latest turn is a refusal, whatever is open, since the next message would go into the conversation the provider refused; after any other error it holds while an order or question is open to the seat. Either hold names the error and raises the ordinary alarm: the status line of every ship session carries it (law: nothing-waits-silently), the systems seat is told, and for a seat in a human session the desk too. A seat in a human session is read from `runtime/seats/<seat>/turn-failure.json`, written by the service from its own reading of the record: kind, code, first words, time and output certainty, bound to the launch and the record's fingerprint, and never an input, reply or subject; a changed record reads as unknown until the service reads it again, so an old refusal never stands as a current fact.


Where a delivery cannot be confirmed, the one look, and `qd submit`, are [the door](door.md#the-door).

What a systems seat may press or type into a stalled crew seat, and what is refused to the desk, is [the door](door.md#the-door).

**Provider refusals.** A crew seat whose current launch's latest turn is a provider refusal is started fresh by the service, once, without the refused message. The service acts only when the turn has ended with a known-empty queue and tool set; an open turn, queued input or a running tool waits with the door held. The refused message is the single ship message the door typed on this launch whose whole envelope is an input of the refused turn. No input, an input the door did not type (a human's text, or the launch's carried first message), an input matching several messages, or several messages in the turn are ambiguous. Once the refused message is named it is quarantined and its sender told, whether or not the fresh start may proceed: an unknown queue or tool set, or a refusal of the successor before its first normal turn, holds only the fresh start, and a fresh start by hand carries every other open message and the refused one as a notice, never its text. A refused message that cannot be named is held, shown and alarmed for the systems seat or a person; nothing is quarantined on a guess, and the status says the refused message is not identified and must be kept out by hand before any `qd fresh`.

`runtime/seats/<seat>/refusal.json` (format 1) keeps one event per refused turn, keyed by the launch's pane, process and time and the turn's end, written durably before any act: the refused message and its sender, the fixed identity and time of the sender's notice, and the stage, `claimed`, `launched` with the successor launch's time, or `held` with its reason. The sender receives a report from `ship` that refers to the refused message, says the seat's provider refused its conversation at it and that it will not be typed again, and quotes none of it; then the ordinary `qd fresh` lifecycle relaunches the seat. A claim that finds a different launch adopts it; one that finds the seat down is held; one whose relaunch fails is held with the failure as its reason, logged once and never retried by itself: the status line says to inspect, then `qd fresh <seat>`, and the systems seat or a person acts. A held claim keeps its quarantine and its notice. A notice already published is recognized by its id and never rebuilt, so a later change of its words never conflicts with a notice an earlier version wrote. Each seat's refusal step runs on its own: a failure of its journal, a notice or its files is logged once while it stands and never stops the next seat's step. A launch begun by an automatic refusal fresh that is itself refused before it completes a turn normally is held for a person: the ship does not start a seat fresh twice in a row for refusals.

The refused message stays open and whole in the store; nothing closes it or calls it delivered. It is withheld from this seat for good: the launch's first message carries it as a one-line notice, its id, kind and sender, `withheld: refused by the provider · sender told`, without its subject or body (law: context-is-cache), the inbox and `qd inbox` show it the same way, no tick types it, and `qd post resend` refuses it with the reason. A reworded message is a new message. The seat may close a withheld order with an ordinary report saying it was not acted on. An unreadable refusal journal holds: a launch refuses to carry anything and the inbox withholds every body until it is inspected.

Seats in a human session get no automatic fresh start; the desk acts, as for every other automatic lifecycle act there. No subscription moves on a refusal. Other programs' errors are shown and alarmed but never start a seat fresh until their refusal form is measured into fixtures.

An older framework rejects the `unacted` and `uncertain` journal phases as unknown and holds, and does not honor `refusal.json`: before rolling back, stop the service, resolve any claimed refusal, and remember that a quarantined message would be carried again.

**The ship's tmux server.** A ship runs on a tmux server of its own, never tmux's default one. Its socket is `runtime/tmux/server` under the ship folder, a fixed path, so qd finds it from the ship folder alone, and git never tracks it. Every command qd sends it names that socket with `-S`; the person's own tmux configuration still loads. No qd path runs a bare `tmux`, and neither `TMUX` nor `TMUX_TMPDIR` chooses the ship's server. Every tmux command qd runs to read or act passes `-u`: a client whose locale is not UTF-8 (LANG, LC_ALL and LC_CTYPE unset or C) prints a format's tabs as `_`, whatever the server's locale, and `-u` keeps them without depending on an installed UTF-8 locale. A pane line not in qd's format raises `TmuxFormatError` with the line; tmux's all-empty line for a pane gone still reads as gone. `qd attach` alone runs without `-u`: it shows tmux to a person, under the person's own locale. qd's pane format carries tmux's `pane_dead_time` beside `pane_dead_status`; the service's accounting pass, which already reads every seat's pane, logs a `pane-dead` event with its death time and exit status. Tmux can mark a pane dead before setting its exit status: launch errors and activity then say `exit status not yet known`. If the first event has an unknown status, a later ordinary accounting pass logs the same pane once more when its status is known, including zero; repeated pending or known readings add no events. This adds no pane query or wait, and retains tmux's death time.

- **`QD_TMUX_SOCKET`.** A value naming another path is honoured only in two cases: for a creator's private namespace, which the tests and the drill use, its folder this user's at mode 700 and its lock this user's mode-600 file with the namespace header; and for the server that holds this ship's sessions while the ship's own holds none. Anything else is refused by name, so one ship never runs on another's server.
- **The socket's place.** qd makes the socket's folder mode 700 when it first starts the server. Before a launch it refuses, changing nothing:
  - a path whose bytes and terminating NUL exceed the 108 a socket's path holds;
  - a folder that is a link, another user's or open to others;
  - anything but this user's socket at the path.
- **`qd attach [session]`** replaces itself with a tmux client on the ship's server. It opens the person's own session by default (the first declared `human = true`, else the first), or the session named by its key or exact title. Inside the ship's own server it switches the client instead; inside another tmux it opens a client of its own.
- **A seat's `launch.sh`** cuts its program off from the server it runs in:
  - it removes `TMUX` and `TMUX_PANE`;
  - it sets `TMUX_TMPDIR` to a fresh folder made for this launch alone, mode 700, under the seat's own `tmux` folder (itself refused unless it is the seat's, mode 700, and no link), so a bare tmux finds no server but one it starts itself. Earlier launches' folders are removed only when empty. Where the ship's folder is so deep that a socket there would be too long, a bare tmux fails with "File name too long" and reaches nothing;
  - it sets `QD_TMUX_SOCKET` to the server the seat runs on, but for a jailed seat, which is told none;
  - it removes, by name, every session mark an adapter declares (`inherited_markers`), except one the seat's own adapter sets in its launch;
  - it removes, by name, what the seat's own adapter declares its program must not inherit (`launch_unset`). Only Grok declares any: `VTE_VERSION`.

  Claude Code's launch environment sets `CLAUDECODE`, `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`, `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION`, `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ATTENDED` and `CLAUDE_PID`, and adds the session's private `CLAUDE_CODE_MESSAGING_SOCKET` and `CLAUDE_CODE_MESSAGING_TOKEN`. At start it reads the first four as a parent session's. Its preferences, sign-in and privacy settings are left alone. With `TMUX` absent, an inherited `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION` turns transcript saving off, so the seat would keep no record (law: one-record). Removing the declared marks keeps the record. Codex reads the same with and without `TMUX`.

  Grok's `VTE_VERSION` is a terminal's mark, not a session's. A ship started from a VTE terminal hands that version on, though a seat's terminal is tmux. Grok reads a VTE of 8200 or later as a terminal that tells Shift+Enter apart, and then draws another hint row under its composer: `Enter:send │ Shift+Enter/Alt+Enter:newline │ Shift+Tab:mode` for an idle draft. An unmeasured hint reads as no box, so a paste from such a ship goes blind (law: one-door). Grok's launch removes `VTE_VERSION`. The reader also knows that one measured row, so a Grok that sees a VTE some other way still reads its idle draft. Every other program keeps the variable.
- **A ship not yet moved.** A ship that ran on tmux's default server before it had its own is found there: by the socket `TMUX` names, and by the default socket under `TMUX_TMPDIR`, or `/tmp` when that is unset. Each is read with `-S` and `-N`, and a session counts as the ship's only by its declared title and its `QD_SHIP`. While its own server holds none of its sessions and a default server holds some, qd keeps running it there and says so:
  - readings, deliveries and launches go where the ship is, every launch with the clean environment and `QD_TMUX_SOCKET` naming that server;
  - the status line names the socket: `tmux: still on the default server <socket>; qd down, then qd up, moves it`, kept in short when the line overflows;
  - `qd down` brings the seats down there and removes only the ship's own sessions once empty, and the next `qd up` starts the ship on its own socket;
  - a service started before the move follows the ship to its own server on its next tick once the default server no longer holds it, and a service is started with a caller's `QD_TMUX_SOCKET` only where the ship's own choice of server accepts it.

  qd never stops the default server, nor the ship's: a ship's server ends with its last session. A ship found in both places starts nothing more until it is in one. The move is one `qd down` and one `qd up`, run from a terminal outside the ship, since a seat's own `qd down` ends that seat.

**Launch retirement.** A down, restart or fresh start retires the saved launch's window before forgetting its launch metadata. A present pane with another PID, a changed saved launch, failed native close or unconfirmed disappearance is a visible refusal; it preserves the evidence and blocks a new launch. The one exception is a launch from an earlier tmux server lifetime: pane ids start again from %0 when the server restarts, as after a reboot, while `launch.json` survives, so another program's new pane can carry a saved id. When the running server's own start (`#{start_time}`, whole seconds) is later than the time `launch.json` was written, the saved pane cannot be in this server, so the launch reads as gone: up, down and fresh proceed, and the other pane is left alone. The file's write time is the bound because it is written after its pane exists; the launch's `at` is stamped before the window opens, and opening it can start the server. An unreadable start, or a restart within the same second as the write, keeps the refusal. The native close queue checks the expected PID in the server performing the kill, so a replacement after the caller's check is retained. A matching failed pane may be retired when up is retried. Closing the owned window ends its program and the ordinary bubblewrap jail's descendants; the process/window regression counts repeated fresh starts and final down. This is no authority to adopt unidentified historical windows, match titles or sweep processes. Native records and provenance remain intact. Closing the pane does not prove the program has ended: a program may outlive its closed pane while it writes its state. So `qd up` records the program's start time from `/proc` in the launch (`start`, beside its pid), for a process of this user's only, and down, after closing the pane, observes that pid with that start time until the process has ended, within `PROCESS_END_SECONDS` (10 s); it sends no signal. A launch without a recorded start, one written before this or a stand-in's, is not waited for: no other process is ever taken for its program. Only then does it reseed the credential and remove the launch, and its proof line says `pane closed · process ended`. Past the bound it says `pane closed · process still ending after 10s; credential and launch left for the next tick`, keeps the launch and writes `retiring.json` (the pid and start) beside it. While that program lives, the tick's credential step reseeds nothing of the seat and `qd up` refuses it; once it has ended, the tick reseeds and removes the launch, and logs `retired`. A relaunch, restart, fresh or a pool move, runs down and up in one lifecycle: past the first bound it keeps observing the same pid and start, never signalling, up to `RELAUNCH_END_SECONDS` (60 s) in all, and up follows as soon as the end is seen; its proof line states both waits. Past that bound the relaunch is refused, `relaunch-refused.json` is written beside the marker, and every ship session's status line names the seat in plain words, that its program is still ending and its relaunch was refused, or, once the tick has retired the program, that its relaunch was refused and `qd up` brings it back, until an up succeeds. An unreadable marker counts as still ending. The tick never relaunches.

Lifecycle callers on the same runtime and seat take one advisory file lock through startup preparation, native retirement, metadata publication/removal and the full restart/fresh/failover stop/select/start sequence. Explicit CLI up, down, restart, fresh and pool selection, and temporary-seat teardown down, wait up to thirty seconds per seat for that claim. They retry only lock acquisition, then re-read the launch and run the transition once; expiry retains the existing busy refusal. Delivery and all service dispatch keep nonblocking claims, including queued explicit relaunch and pool-selection requests; a busy request is answered or deferred without delaying the remaining tick. Independent seats remain independent. The empty `runtime/post/lifecycle/<seat>.lock` inode is retained outside removable seat homes, never unlinked by lifecycle cleanup, and its non-inheritable descriptor releases on failure or process exit. It carries no ownership, recovery or configuration authority and changes no launch/Proof/record schema. Sync operations must quiesce old lifecycle callers: older binaries ignore this new coordination file, so mixed old/new lifecycle operations are unsupported. All participating callers must preserve the lock inode; external replacement/removal is outside this advisory protocol.

**A relaunch a stop waits out.** Every relaunch the service makes runs in one section, the same way a delivery does: a fresh start after an order, a relaunch at a request, a handoff or a renewed sign-in (kind `relaunch`), a fresh start after a provider refusal, a move at a wall or by `qd set`, a fresh start after compactions, and the bring-up of each seat after the ship's tmux server was lost. Before the down, the service writes the relaunch's intent, `runtime/post/relaunch/<seat>.json`, naming itself, and logs a `relaunch` line with phase `begin`. After the up it removes the intent and logs phase `done` (or `failed`). A stop that arrives inside the section waits for the up and then exits. The stopper waits up to 30 seconds from SIGTERM while that service has an intent open, the same bound a delivery has. The crew's measured ups took 6 to 15 seconds after the down had closed the pane, and the bound is twice the slowest of them. A section still open at the bound is taken as hung. The stopper kills the service, marks the intent released, logs `relaunch-released`, and names the seat in its own result. The intent stays on the status line until the next service settles it.

The next service settles each intent first in its pass, before anything else reads the seat. It reads the intent without following a link (`post_dir`), and refuses it whole, once in the log and on the status line, when it is a link, unreadable, not an intent the service wrote, or names a seat the ship does not declare. It never acts on an intent whose service is still running. Under the seat's lifecycle lock it clears an intent whose seat is up, or which an up has already followed. Otherwise it marks the intent `finishing` and finishes the up once, through `Seat._up` like any other launch, so a jailed seat's folder check runs again. Then it removes the intent and logs the finish. A finish that fails, or that is itself cut short, is never tried again: the status line says `needs a human: qd up <seat>`, and the intent is cleared once the seat is up.

An intent never overrides a later word that the seat stays as it is. A person's `qd down` (one seat or the whole ship), `qd stop` or `qd fresh` removes the seat's intent before it acts, and logs it as `cleared by qd down by <seat or operator>`. So does each service hold that keeps a seat down, as it is placed: a refusal hold, a relaunch refused while its program was ending, a stopped ship-server recovery (every seat's), and a jailed seat stopped at its write bound (`write_bound`). A later hold registers a reader in `relaunch_intent.HOLDS` and calls `relaunch_intent.clear` where it is placed. The finish also leaves an intent as it is while anything holds the seat: `up = false` in the ship file, `recovery.held`, a refusal hold, a refused relaunch, a program still ending, a stopped server recovery, or a registered hold. A hold that cannot be read counts as a hold. The status line says `<seat>: relaunch intent left as it is while held: <reason>`.

**Failover.** A seat's `pool` may be one name or an ordered list. Strings retain the seat's default harness, model, effort and settings. A list may also contain explicit choices such as `{pool="alternate", harness="claude", model="chosen-model", effort="high", settings={}}`. Every inline choice names its own harness and nonempty model; omitted effort/settings use that program's defaults rather than the seat's original program's options. The adapter validates pool compatibility. Model names are declared, not checked against a remote catalog at load time; the installed program must accept them. Duplicate pool names, malformed choices and incompatible harness/pool pairs are refused.

At a wall, the current owned record must explicitly show no open turn, an error-free known empty queue and a complete known empty pending-tool set, plus a timed completed turn with known start/end timestamps spanning its latest timestamped active input. Empty or metadata-only records, inferred accounting endings and child-only completions supply no terminal proof; an earlier completion cannot cover a later input whose terminal timestamp is missing. Missing, unreadable or unknown evidence holds even if the screen looks idle; a seat without explicit active-conversation terminal proof cannot fail over. The service tries later choices in declared order, then earlier choices from the start of the list. It uses the destination adapter's existing usage and freshness rules for each candidate. It skips walled candidates and candidates with unknown capacity. If none qualify, the seat stays at its visible wall. This also applies to human-owned seats. A seat with room stays on its current pool, including when an earlier pool resets.

One ordinary live idle predicate supplies failover, explicit selection and explicit relaunch, with explicit recovery's narrow residual queue-error exception. Relaunch still needs a known empty queued collection, no pending tools and the latest input's real timed completion. A `queue_error` alone does not veto that explicit restart or fresh forever after a real completion; a queued or unknown collection still holds. Automatic failover and selection retain the stricter error-free queue rule. The separate explicit question-only recovery exception is unchanged. A refusal names the missing record, turn, queue, tool or completion dimension. At a wall the held-move reason appears even when a destination has room, and retains the seat and dimension when the status line overflows. Observed JSON call start times survive accounting turn closure; a pending call can say its name and original start, or that the start is unknown. This detail does not establish completeness: only the reader's complete pending-tool set can prove empty. An ordinary tool result, later turn completion, new prompt, elapsed time or idle screen does not prove an opaque background command ended. The plain activity column of `qd status` and the status reading says only idle or working, as before: a record that never proves its tools would otherwise read as tool state unknown on nearly every seat. The tool reason appears in `qd watch`'s full seat reading and in each hold it causes, where it is why a relaunch, move or control waits.

The service writes a disposable move-decision fact containing its category and launch/watch generation. Human status-line collection reads this minimal fact and owned launch/watch metadata, with no new conversation read; crew facts may additionally show a bounded call label and recorded start, with no arguments, output or native call identity. A changed launch/watch, an absent or malformed fact, or a decision older than five seconds reads move proof unknown. Unchanged facts refresh no more often than every 2.5 seconds; category/detail changes write immediately. Wall decisions are retried by the existing service pass, and a fresh clear decision retires the hold. These presentation facts never authorize a move.

Claude records a root Bash background task when its joined tool result supplies `toolUseResult.backgroundTaskId`, including a foreground command promoted after timeout. The returned handle starts the task's evidence and does not end it. A terminal notification must match both the root call id and the background task id, with status `completed`, `failed` or `killed`, on a measured carrier: queue enqueue or remove, a user task-notification origin, or a `queued_command` attachment of that origin. A successful joined `TaskStop` ends its matching `local_bash` task; a failed stop ends nothing. A running status, a summary or an exit-code sentence is not completion. The Monitor tool's event notice is that Monitor's own, by its tool-use id or the task id its result names, and never settles a Bash task. A notification the reader cannot parse holds only itself: a tracked Bash task it names by either identity becomes unknown, and it changes nothing else, since an unread notice can never end a task. An untracked Bash task, or a TaskStop the reader does not track, keeps the record's tool state unknown. Claude Code 2.1.317 is the measured set for TaskStop, queue-remove, the attachment carriers and the paste transforms. 2.1.322 is trusted for the background call and its result, the queue-enqueue and user-row notification carriers and a running task status, and for nothing else. A call and its result must name the same version; any other version stays unknown. Unversioned queue carriers use the nearest earlier explicit version, because one record can span resumed program versions.

A user-row task notification is not a typed prompt. The adapter excludes the known `task-notification` envelope from `last_input`, input history and turn accounting. A notice after a completed turn leaves that turn closed. An assistant row after it opens a turn at that row's time. A resumed `stopped` notice with a note leaves tools unknown, so the idle proof still holds even though plain activity reads idle. A `killed` notice whose note, after the summary, is exactly Claude's own note that it stopped the command under memory pressure while the session was idle ends the task on the queue-enqueue and user carriers only. Any other note stays unknown. A notification written before its call's own rows, as Claude writes it when the inbox watch returns at once because mail is waiting, is held by its root call id and joins when that call's result arrives later in the same file. One that never meets its root call leaves the record's tools unknown.

The seat's own registered inbox watch is the sole running-work exception, and only when the background command is exactly `qd inbox --wait`. The decision also needs the current registration, an unchanged watch generation and launch digest, the same process start, and live ancestry under that launch. The registration must not predate the native call. Exactly one candidate can be excluded. The registration is a pid and a time. Optional `watch-proof.json` binds those bytes to the process start, the launch digest and the canonical `watch-generation` token, the same generation the waking line uses. Retirement removes only its own unchanged registration and companion. Proof reads those files, the launch metadata and at most 64 process ancestors, and never a command line, an environment or a credential. An explicit current join of `no record yet`, with a live pane matching the saved launch, is admitted as **no record yet: nothing has run** for relaunch, controls and ordinary moves. A generic absent record, an unknown join or an unproved pane still holds. An interrupt during publication retires that same registration. A retirement error cannot replace the wait's result.

**Queued explicit requests.** A single request owner coordinates claim, dispatch and settlement with cancellation and supersession. A client publishes the original request once under seat admission, without holding dispatch coordination; a paused publisher cannot block healthy requests. The service retires older same-seat relaunches when it reads a newer request, before dispatch. Equal or unreadable creation order keeps every candidate visible and withdrawable with a named hold; it never archives one to make another eligible. A claimed older request likewise holds its replacement with an unknown-outcome reason. An unapplied seat request keeps its kind, creation time and the service's precise current hold category/reason. The command's timeout answer, `qd status` and `qd watch` show that metadata and the request id without reading a conversation or revealing control arguments. The status line names it immediately as `<seat> <kind> request waits: <reason>`, including unknown holds and overflow: its alert bound is zero seconds. Request ids and cancellation commands stay in the detailed status and watch readings. Other crew requests remain deferred and retry on the existing service pass, with no new loop. A request begun on another launch is refused rather than applied to its successor.

`qd restart SEAT --cancel REQUEST` and `qd fresh SEAT --cancel REQUEST` withdraw that seat's pending request by id, including a pending control. Without an id, `--cancel` withdraws all its pending relaunches. Both are the same request-owner act and print the settled identities; no separate request-list API is added. Cancellation needs no native program resolution and works when the service is down. A direct restart or fresh settles earlier pending relaunches before admitting its replacement, and its proof names them. The owner writes a replay guard and a terminal retirement intent before publishing the ordinary `.done` answer and atomically retiring the active name, so an interrupted cancellation recovers without dispatch. A claimed request with unknown outcome remains visible and is never guessed to be cancelled or replayed. An older service sees the retained claim and cannot replay a partially settled cancellation.

Before dispatch, the service records its PID and process start with the claim's payload digest in request metadata. At the next service start, a claim whose exact payload still matches and whose claiming process is proved gone is settled without dispatch: the asker receives `settled without applying: the service that claimed it restarted`, and the service logs the settlement. PID reuse counts as a departed claimer only when the recorded process start differs. A live or unreadable process identity stays held; legacy claims need the separate timestamp proof below. Explicit cancellation also accepts a claim with a proved-departed owner. Nothing settled is applied or replayed; an interrupted settlement retains its terminal intent for recovery.

For legacy claims without owner metadata, startup also accepts a strict filesystem-time proof. After publishing its new owner under the exclusive service lock, it captures that lock file's ctime as its start boundary. A matching claim on the same filesystem must have a ctime more than the shared `post.POST_TIMESTAMP_TICK_NS` (two seconds) before that boundary, using the supported filesystem tick documented in [Post](post.md). Claims at or after the boundary, within that tick, on another filesystem, or with invalid owner metadata stay held. Existing PID/start provenance takes precedence. This legacy proof applies only to automatic service-start settlement; cancellation without it continues to require a proved-departed process.

The known-idle direct CLI path retains its existing manual-recovery admission (`is_working`), without silently adding the service's timed-latest-input proof. The same request-owner coordination covers both retiring old queued relaunches and executing that existing direct act. A working seat still files its relaunch with the service and receives the updated queued proof and human deferral policy. This retained explicit manual boundary is distinct from permission for an automatic move or a deferred request.

A live human-session relaunch from another caller is never deferred: it either applies at the immediate proved break or is refused with its reason and a terminal answer. An expired request, a completion after the request was made, or later input cannot become a delayed restart. A self-request is the seat's own only when its filer descends from that seat's own pane process: at enqueue the filing process and its parents are read from /proc until the pane process the launch records, by its pid and its start time, so a reused pid never counts. Naming the seat as caller without that descent is refused as not the seat's own, with the reason, and nothing is filed. The asked time is stamped at enqueue, never taken from the filer. The service counts a request as the seat's own only with that proof, for the launch the seat still runs; any other request is another caller's. A self-request uses the current launch and the person's latest classified input. Only the person's later typing retires it: the fixed waking line, adapter-declared notices and whole known delivery forms for messages with a recorded attempt at this seat do not. No prefix or partial payload is classified as a delivery. Framework work can advance the native input and turn identities without retiring the request; it waits for the next proved quiet break, with no open turn, a timed end after the latest input, no queued or unconfirmed delivery and an empty idle box, then the existing shared relaunch proof still decides. An adapter without classified person-input evidence retains the exact-asking-turn rule: a self-request made during its own recorded turn may wait for that exact turn's end, bound to the launch and input/turn evidence, rather than firing after an unproved later break. A self-request for a fresh start made once the seat has posted an open order to itself, its handoff, is bound to that handoff instead: a delivery or program notice that reaches the seat later is an open message the fresh start carries, so it never retires the request, which applies at the next quiet moment: no turn open and the latest turn's end proved, nothing queued, every delivery to the seat confirmed, and the box read empty and idle. The person's own later input, read as the quiet time reads it, or the handoff's closing retires it. Where the person's input cannot be told from the program's notices, the asking-turn rule above decides. The separate explicit question-only recovery remains unchanged. In-session controls retain their existing working-turn admission and use the shared own-watch-adjusted tool evidence; they do not inherit the ordinary move's timed-completion or queue predicate. Human policy and lifecycle permission checks require the independent Claude gate.

Original request payloads and names, `.done` publication/acknowledgment and `.claimed` bytes stay unchanged. Optional creation/wait, asking-turn and terminal-retirement facts live beside them in `runtime/request-state/NAME.state`; requests without caller context keep their original single payload publication and use its file time until the first held observation preserves it there. A complete `.done` answer is published before the original log and active-name retirement order, and its client consumes it without waiting for metadata cleanup. Metadata retires with the request or its acknowledgment. The writer coordination file is `runtime/request.lock`, outside the acknowledgment spool, so a completed request leaves no new file in that spool; status/watch read metadata without creating it. The service attempts coordination without waiting and defers a busy request to the next tick. CLI cancellation and direct admission wait at most `ANSWER_SECONDS`, then refuse with a busy-owner reason. Direct acts hold coordination only for retirement and the working recheck; the seat transition runs afterwards under its existing lifecycle lock. Service dispatch also releases coordination after its saved claim, which excludes cancellation and supersession until settlement; no seat transition holds request coordination. A reading never repairs an unreadable request. These are runtime-only facts, not ship-file settings or persisted conversation copies.

`qd set <seat> pool=<choice>` on a seat with inline choices asks the service to move it to that declared choice, including while the current pool has room. The service uses the same move path, idle proof and destination reading as failover. An explicit choice may move to a pool whose usage is unknown, and the answer says so. The service immediately refuses an undeclared or walled destination, a held or unresolved selection, or a seat without the terminal proof above, and gives the reason. A seat in its own turn cannot select itself, so the desk or another seat issues it. The selection lives in runtime state.

A down seat has no turn to wait for: `qd set <seat> pool=<choice>` writes the choice at once, marked as a person's, and the seat's next launch takes it. A seat whose last launch is still on file, its pane gone, is refused until `qd down` retires that launch. At every other launch of a down seat, by `qd up` or `qd up <seat>`, the ship file is the Admiral's order: the seat takes the first declared choice with room, one the ledger does not show walled. A saved choice later in the list than a choice with room gives way, and so does one the ship file no longer declares, which a launch reads as absent while every reading still names it. With no choice that has room, the saved choice stays. A move to another program starts the seat fresh, with its open messages, as failover does. `qd up <seat> --pool <choice>` takes a declared choice for that one launch. The launch's proof line names the pool and why, and the saved choice it replaced.

Before changing selection, the source join/reader/provenance is retained and any refreshed credential reseeds its original paying pool. The existing pool file records the destination. Same-program moves restart with resume; different programs start fresh with the current program's brief and every open message in full, using ordinary lifecycle receipts. A carried message gains a launch receipt, not proof that an old uncertain submission was delivered. Uncarried pending journals stay held against their original identity. Historical records retain their own adapters and pool boundaries. Adapters regenerate their program configuration from declared templates/options in the existing seat home; the framework does not reinterpret the prior program's record or settings.

The saved selection and launch identify the program after ticks/restarts. Before stopping a seat with inline choices, the existing atomic pool file retains its corroborated selection while the saved launch still exists, including when choices were added to a running seat. A failed selection write leaves that launch and pane intact. A known matching launch can resolve a missing selection file, but historical reader/paying-pool journals alone do not prove a current choice. An unknown, unreadable or conflicting selection stays refused by lifecycle actions rather than guessed.

Status/watch rows retain an unresolved seat as unknown with its reason and unknown current pool/program, without reading a record or screen under a guessed adapter. Other seats remain readable. The service logs/notifies that seat's hold and continues healthy duties, receipts and requests; its old uncertainty is neither confirmed nor resent. Re-briefing retains the unresolved work rather than claiming completion. Readings do not create a selection file. The JSON facts add an `unknown` list for compact selection reasons; crowded status lines retain a selection-unknown count and directions to the full reading. Later briefs, doctor checks and live-check defaults use a resolved pool choice. Status rows show the current program/model/pool; the status line names seats on a later pool, or counts them when crowded. Existing ship notices/logs record each completed move.

For a declaration containing inline choices, edit harness, model, effort and program settings explicitly in `ship.toml`. `qd set` refuses those options (including `settings.KEY` and bare program keys), and `qd set --adopt` is unavailable: neither can choose between the seat defaults and a pool entry. Common seat options such as `up`, `cwd` and `pool`, and controls/adoption on legacy string-only declarations, retain their existing behavior.

Automatic after-order fresh and failover retain their separate completion/queue gates; no question wait becomes a terminal turn or a delivery receipt. These are read-side values and generated native settings, with no new ledger layout or persisted execution store.


How a paste is checked and cleared, and how a message is typed into a seat that is mid-turn, is [the door](door.md#the-door).

**A seat's home.** Each program keeps its settings, instruction files, transcripts and logs under one home directory named by one variable: `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` for Claude Code, `CODEX_HOME` for Codex, `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR` for pi, the XDG directories for OpenCode. The user's own home for a program is the user's, and a ship seat never runs in it. Quarterdeck builds every seat a home of its own under the runtime, from files it owns: the program's settings as the ship wants them, from the framework's template or the ship's own `homes/<harness>/` folder in the program's own format, with the seat's settings laid over; trust for the workspace and the first-run flags already set, so a fresh home opens without a dialog; the program's self-update and its own memory turned off where it has the switches, since a seat keeps nothing of its own between conversations and what must survive is a post or a file of the ship's; permission for `qd post` granted through the program's own permissions; and nothing of the user's. The pool's credential is the one thing shared: an API key by the environment variable the program reads; a subscription by environment where the program takes a long-lived token there, by a link to the pool's one credential file otherwise, or by a copy that the service keeps in step where the program writes through that link; and never by a copy left to itself, because refresh tokens are single use and a copied credential dies at the first refresh, which has logged most of a crew out at once. The link alone is not enough either: Claude Code writes a refreshed credential by atomic rename, which turns the seat's link into a private file holding the only live refresh token and strands the pool's file and every other seat on it within seconds. So the service watches every seat's credential file: when one stops being a link, a refresh happened there, and the service copies the refreshed file over the pool's, parks the stale one beside it, and relinks the seat, before the next seat needs it. The renewal retires the token every other seat still holds, so the look cannot wait for the pass, which can be minutes apart: a dedicated watch in the service looks at each linked seat's credential name twice a second (names only, never contents) and reseeds at once, so every other seat's next read finds the renewal within two seconds. Claude Code writes its renewal as a complete temporary beside the name, renamed over it, so the watch sees a whole file. A shared writable folder in place of the link was weighed and refused: the program's sign-in lives at one fixed name in its own home, so sharing it would share the whole home, and a folder every seat writes would let one jailed seat plant a sign-in for all. Every reseed with something to judge (a linked name no longer a link, or a held copy whose size or time differs from the pool's), the watch's, the pass's and a seat's own at down, holds one lock file, `runtime/locks/credential-reseed.lock`, so two never install at once. Every jail hides `runtime/locks` behind an empty private tmpfs, since `flock` needs no write access and a jailed seat holding the lock would stop the pass. The pass and the watch wait for it at most two seconds, then skip the round, log it and retry on the next; a seat going down waits at most thirty. Grok Build writes through the link, so two of its seats refreshing at once would race on the account holder's own sign-in, and a refused refresh written last would sign out the account holder and every seat together. Its seats hold a copy, never a link: each refresh lands in the seat's own home, the tick puts the newest live grant in the pool's place as above, and brings every copy older than the pool's up to it by one atomic exchange (`renameat2` with `RENAME_EXCHANGE`), so the program never finds the place empty and a refresh it wrote in the meantime is swapped straight back and installed on the next pass. Building a home keeps a copy fresher than the pool's, since it is that seat's own refresh. The tick is not the only reseed: a seat going down reseeds from its own home once its program is gone and before its launch, which names the pool the refresh belongs to, is forgotten, so a restart or a check's throwaway seat keeps a refresh with no service running; and a seat's home is never built over a detached credential fresher than its pool's, which is refused and kept for the human, since it may hold the only live token. The grant itself also expires, about every four weeks, and no relink cures that, only the human's login into the pool; the service reads the credential's expiry times as metadata, never the token values, and the status line warns two days ahead: `claude-secondary · login needed by 11-13 22:24`. What an expiry means depends on the grant's kind, which each adapter declares from its measured facts. A fixed grant, such as Claude Code's, whose `refreshTokenExpiresAt` ends the grant that renews its access, warns as above. A self-renewing grant, such as Grok Build's, carries a short access expiry that the program renews on use, so it passes every few hours with no login needed and never warns: the line warns only when the renewal has visibly failed (`login needed by now (its renewal failed)`), or when the grant that renews it is readable by a named field and within two days of its end. An unknown kind (Codex, Antigravity, OpenCode and pi, for none of which a grant's renewal or end has been measured) is never counted; `qd doctor` says `grant kind unknown` in the pool's detail, and for a self-renewing grant names its current access time and whether the end of its renewing grant is known. So a ship seat and the user's own sessions share a subscription and nothing else: not settings, not instruction files, not memory, not transcripts, not logs. What follows: the programs' logs are in the runtime; a seat's record is the only record of its launch in its home, so the join is exact by construction; the user's own directory stops filling with the crew's sessions; and the ship's program settings are files in the ship folder, read and changed like everything else of the ship's. The pane itself is not logged: a program redraws its screen many times a second, and the record already holds every word.

**The ship's locks.** `flock` needs no write access: a descriptor opened read-only takes an exclusive lock, so any lock file a jailed seat can open is one it can hold. Every lock the service or a `qd` command waits on therefore lies under `runtime/locks`, which every jail hides behind an empty private tmpfs (`ship_locks.py`). That includes the locks once taken on a data file itself, such as the service log's append, a seat's record registry and launch journal (which that seat's own jail can see), and the ledger's opening, once taken on the runtime folder. The files those locks guard stay where they are, and the ship no longer locks them. Every wait is bounded: thirty seconds, or ten minutes for a copy waiting on another copy; past it the caller gets the lock's name and skips or refuses, as the service's pass does for any failing step, and tries again later. Inside a jail no service lock is visible, so `qd` reads a heartbeat younger than two minutes as a running service. The lock's earlier place, `runtime/service.lock`, still counts as held while it exists, because during a sync the earlier code's service may hold it; the first service of this code removes it, and a held one refuses the start by name. The install lock's earlier place is treated the same way. The cascade's notes are appended under a lock of this kind too; every file the cascade and the groups read is published whole by rename, so a reader takes no lock and writes nothing. One lock stays in reach: a temporary check seat's lease under `post/transient/`, which the check seat's own jailed `qd inbox` must read. Every take of it is non-blocking, so nothing waits on it. A foreign jail holding it could make a check refuse as busy, or make a dead check's registration read as live; it cannot stall the service or any delivery.

**A jail's own folder steers nothing outside its seat.** A jailed seat can write its whole folder, `runtime/seats/<seat>`, home included. So the service reads every file there as that seat's own claim. Nothing in it may file a post under another name, install a pool's credential, charge another pool, or stop a step for another seat.

- A saved journal never publishes a message taken whole from the file:
  - The alarm journals (`human-alert.json`, `unconfirmed-delivery-alert.json`, `selection-alert.json`, `manual-fresh-alert.json`) publish only a report from ship, to a recipient the ship file names for alarms (a systems seat, the desk, or the caller's own list). From the file the service keeps only the checked id and recipient. The body is this pass's own reading of the alarm, or, for the manual-fresh alarm, the first observation's words when every handoff they name is a real self-handoff in the store. Across a changed or cleared reading, the old words exist only in the file, and are not published.
  - `compaction.json` publishes only the protocol's own messages, each checked against the service's words: the threshold request, the seat's attestation report, and the seat's own handoff to itself in reply to that request.
  - `refusal.json` builds each notice from the refused message in the store. That message must be addressed to this seat and sent by a declared seat, its code a short token and its turn a time.

  Anything else is refused, logged once (`alarm-refused`, `compaction-intent-refused`, `refusal-notice-refused`), and never published.
- No write of the service or of qd into the folder follows a link the jail planted (`seat_files`; `durable.replace_file` takes the same path there):
  - each folder on the way is opened without following a link, and a link where a folder belongs refuses the write;
  - a file, `launch.sh` and the harnesses' home files among them, is written to a temporary created with O_CREAT|O_EXCL|O_NOFOLLOW and renamed over its name, so a planted link is replaced, never followed;
  - a credential link is made under a temporary name and renamed over its name; a credential copy, and the copy a reseed brings up by exchange, are made the same way;
  - an append (`acts.jsonl`, `acts-notices.jsonl`, `record-readers.jsonl`, `record-launches.jsonl`) refuses a link;
  - a home file read to be merged and written back (Claude's `.claude.json`, the owned conversations) reads a link as nothing, so no operator's file is copied into the jail's reach.

  The folder move later takes these files out of the jail's reach altogether.
- The launch record is read whole (`seat.parse_launch`). An unknown, missing or mistyped field refuses it (`LaunchUnreadable`), and that seat alone is held.
- A credential is taken, and relinked, only from a seat whose launch names a pool the ship file declares for it, and never from a jailed seat or a seat on an endpoint pool (`credential.source_refusal`).
- For a jailed seat, accounting, walls and usage take only a pool the ship file declares for it. Its `record-launches.jsonl` is filtered the same way. Its turns on any other pool count unattributed, and the line names the refused record. For an unjailed seat, accounting and walls keep charging the pool its own launch names, so a harness or pool switch still charges the running launch's turns to its old pool. That seat already holds the operator's power, and the threat is a jail writing its own folder. This narrows the security review's term 2 to jailed seats for accounting and walls, by the engineer's decision; credentials judge every seat.
- deliveries, follow, fresh_after_order and refresh_usage guard each seat, and so does account_records for its launch record. One seat's bad file holds that seat alone: it is logged once and named on the status line from `runtime/seat-holds.json`, which only the service writes, outside every seat folder. The status line guards each seat the same way.

How a delivery is stopped in the middle of a paste or a submit, including the waking reservation, is [the door](door.md#the-door).

**Temporary seats.** `seat.temporary_seat` owns the lifetime shared by `qd check` and `qd doctor --fix`. Each caller supplies its own specification and registers only the orders it opens. Ownership is held before any transport through teardown; the service leaves an active owner alone and abandons orphaned unresolved attempts after that owner dies. Teardown retires unresolved transport before removing the home, attempts every cleanup even after an exception, closes only registered orders, and kills only the session it created. Existing seats, folders, history and sessions are refused, not adopted. Posts, attempt receipts and journals survive cleanup; confirmed delivery is never relabelled abandonment. Failure to retire transport retains the home for inspection. The home is removed only once the seat's down has proved its program ended, as `qd down` proves it: a launch still standing, or the launch's process still alive, keeps the home with its credential and record. While teardown runs, SIGINT, SIGTERM and SIGHUP wait until it is done and then act, so a second Ctrl-C or a closing terminal cannot cut a down short. `qd check` takes TERM and HUP as Ctrl-C: an exception where the check stands, so its seat is taken down on every way out, pass, fail, an exception or an interruption. Only a check killed outright (KILL) runs nothing. Its seat then outlives it: a `check-<program>-<run>` home with a launch, whose transient lock no running check holds, is named in the status line of every ship session, with whether its program still runs, until it is gone (law: nothing-waits-silently). `qd down check-<program>-<run>` takes such a seat down through qd, with no tmux by hand: under the same transient lock, refused while its check still runs, it runs the check's own teardown, retiring its transport, downing it, ending its `qd-check-…` session, closing the orders still open to it and removing its home and stale registration, and keeps the home and the name if its program still has not ended. For `qd check` only, `temporary_seat(..., check_inbox=True)` publishes a bounded registration beside its held transient lock, holds its exclusive registration lease before transport through teardown, and then removes that registration. It binds runtime, exact seat, the check-owned human flag, and both registration/transient-lock device/inode identities; owner PID/start are optional diagnostics, not admission authority. Only the inbox resolver admits that otherwise undeclared seat; the generic seat/config/control resolver is unchanged. Admission opens those same regular inodes and tests each lease non-blockingly without retaining a lock. A successful attempt releases immediately and refuses the named stale registration; busy is live lease evidence. Replaced files or unreadable/missing registration refuse by name. Admission does not query the outer owner through `/proc` or depend on its PID namespace. It uses the already-visible writable post mount without widening any mount; the real namespace gate remains separate from source preparation. Missing visibility is a refusal, not permission to add a mount. This is check liveness within a crew whose inboxes are not secret, not a new security boundary. Names alone never authorize a seat or human watch. Nonhuman temporary seats can read their inbox but cannot use `--wait`; the check must explicitly make the seat human. Temporary watch registration, wait iterations and output/receipt boundaries revalidate ownership; existing declared-seat and operator watch behavior is unchanged. The registration is operational lifetime evidence, not another ship declaration, and `ship.toml` is never written for a check.

**Claude's default footer.** The adapter puts a `statusLine` command in each private Claude home before applying the ship's `homes/claude/settings.json` and seat settings. A ship's own command therefore still wins. The command names the framework's Python and packaged `harness/claude_statusline.py` by shell-quoted absolute paths; checkout and plain wheel installs use the same script with `-I -S` and only the standard library. The script reads Claude's stdin model, effort and context facts, plus the declared seat and pools in `ship.toml`. It never changes the ship's declaration or the person's files.

The footer reads `qd --json ledger` and `qd --json status` in a detached background process, through the ordinary qd entry point and installed-copy selection. It makes no provider request itself. Its disposable `runtime/statusline/readings.json` keeps only window display facts, window-error flags, names of pools with usage attempts or historical launches, pool kinds and seat-to-pool names; a process-held lock serializes renewal. Ledger attempts are throttled to thirty seconds and seat-pool attempts to two minutes, including failures. A failed renewal retains the last measurement and marks it `?`. A subscription gets a cell only when this ship uses it and there is an actual window or per-pool usage-attempt reading. Used means that a current seat declares the pool (single, list or inline choice), or the ship's own `record_launches` Ledger table names a launch on it. Background refresh queries only distinct pool names through a read-only connection with a short lock timeout; it neither reads native records nor migrates the book. Missing/unreadable launch evidence grants no new history, while a failed read retains already cached historical use. Background usage polling alone does not count as ship use. Unread used pools and all never-used pools are omitted, including an unread current pool. A failed or incomplete attempted read of a used pool remains `unknown`, never zero. A failed global ledger refresh alone invents no per-pool attempt. Detailed status and ledger retain every declared pool. Endpoint and key pools get no cell. Its short name is the part after the first hyphen, with hyphens replaced by spaces and title case; provider declaration order, then pool name, fixes the same order for every seat. The own-pool marker reserves equal space in every eligible cell.

The largest complete ungrouped blocking window leads the whole-allowance bar, with provider wall evidence taking precedence; a reported unambiguous grouped constraint adds a notch and its figure in brackets only when tighter. Group names are opaque: several different groups do not establish which model's constraint belongs on the notch, and a grouped-only reading is a known constraint without an invented whole-allowance bar. Complete grouped readings do not create an `unknown` marker merely because no total or model match was reported. No model-family matching, window-name guesses or session-rate override supplies missing ledger evidence. Failed or incomplete sibling windows remain `+ unknown` at every detail level. Reset text is `4d 22h`, prefixed `back in` only for a measured wall or 100% usage; an expired retained reset says `reset passed`. The full identity and allowances use one row when they fit, otherwise two, shedding reset times, bars and then all but the tighter figure. When two rows cannot retain every eligible subscription or necessary uncertainty notice, extra rows preserve the facts. Width comes from an explicit environment setting, `COLUMNS`, or the controlling terminal, without an unscoped tmux command; display width counts wide and combining characters.

Ordinary delivery and a seat's lifecycle share one claim for that seat; delivery never waits for it. Its delivery batch,
receipt reconciliation, one look and explicit submit hold the claim only for that seat's work; another
seat's delivery cannot delay its relaunch. A paste reservation requires a complete matching pane, process
and launch. A missing or changed binding leaves the message unread, without a delivery journal.

Before retirement discards a launch, its unresolved ordinary journals retain that launch's existing
one-record join. The final join is checked again after the program ends. Ordinary reconciliation settles
the journal and its attempts from an exact positive receipt there, ahead of a later context-only carry marker. Neither a new launch
nor a different record supplies missing historical evidence. The service separately retires intact
ordinary legacy journals whose only defect is null identity fields: the original bytes stay in a
`retired` subfolder, unresolved matching attempts become unknown and never resent, and one saved
service-log entry records the retirement. Separately owned recovery, receipt-refresh and paste-cleanup
evidence and malformed journals remain held. Readers and strict journal validation do not repair them.
